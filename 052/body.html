What Mattered, Ranked

From 2026-11-06 to 2026-11-10 we made a livelit's slider in Hazel fast enough to drag. Of everything we changed, what made the difference, how much did each change help, and in what order could the team redo it by hand, in small steps? Matthew, 2026-11-10: "a new report that summarizes our work over the last several days, with plots of the current status, and the more significant ingredients, not every single thing we tried in order. Rank them by how much they ended up helping." The full record, build by build, is in reports 050 and 051; this report keeps only what mattered.

Outline

  1. Where it stands: a slider's step on the Witch slide shows in the picture in about 20 to 30 ms, a drag runs at 55 to 58 frames a second, and the page still does 170 to 180 ms of its own work after each step.
  2. The ingredients, ranked: ten changes, ranked by the time each took off when it landed, in two groups: what a user waits for, and the page's own work after the picture.
  3. What is left: the edit made after the paint, the worker's answer two seconds later, and a pause once a step.
  4. Redoing it by hand: an order of increments, each small enough to review, with its size, its tests and what it needs first.
  5. The builds this report cites, what next, and how this was put together.

The ingredients, ranked

Two changes did most of it: sending the worker's answer without what the page did not read (2.8 to 4.8 s off a step, when it landed), and compiling the slide's whole run to JavaScript (1.1 to 1.5 s more, and the reason a step shows in about 20 ms today). Of the page's own work after the picture, keeping the livelits a change did not touch helped most, 0.19 to 0.40 s a step. The ranking is by the time each change took off when it landed, on the measure it changed, as reports 045 to 051 measured it. The measures differ: a step's picture is what a user waits for after a click; a drag's frame is what they see while dragging, 60 times a second; the page's work after the picture is what can make the next frame late. So the ranking is in two groups, and a gain in one is not added to a gain in the other.

{{fig_ranked}}

Three things to keep in mind when reading it. First, a gain can shrink once another lands: the worker's answer was the step's whole wait until the run was compiled, and now it arrives in the background. Second, the ranges are over a build's steps and over one run each; machines differ by up to a third from run to run (report 050 ran build 7 three times: 26.2, 26.4 and 34.3 fps). Third, the drag's 80 to 85 ms is per frame, at up to 60 frames a second, so for a drag it matters more than its place in the list.

Left out of the ranking, because each helped by a few milliseconds or less: the run's first step drawing only what it changed (build 18), the probes' list kept when no probe is removed (build 29), a cache hit taking the new map (build 38), and the outline's mark (build 41) are in the table under the ingredient they belong to; timers that only measured (builds 21, 25, 27, 28, 35 and 40) changed nothing, and are not counted.

What is left

The largest piece of a step's work left is the edit made after the paint, 75 to 90 ms on a fast machine, and it is under way as ticket 721. Build 42 timed the edit's parts: the zipper's own action (writing the model's text into the program) 11 to 20 ms; the statics 14 to 35 ms, of which re-analyzing the edited top-level item is 12 to 28 and parsing 2 to 8; the syntax cache 11 to 29; and the view after it 25 to 38. The zipper's action looked for the livelit's projector by walking every one of the program's 43,171 pieces; build 43 walks only the pieces whose measured extent holds it, and the action takes {{b43_zip}} ms. A model can change the program's values but not its types or its outline, so the statics need not be worked out again; but keeping them is not a matter of skipping a step. A livelit reads its own model from its use's statics record, which holds the use's term, so that record, its elaborated term and the program's elaboration, which the worker runs, would all need the new model, and every record above it holds a term with the old one. Build 44 (1ea585bea8 to d90a8cf135, on a branch of its own) does that: a commit that changes only a model's literals keeps the model's ids, and the statics are patched along the path from each literal up, in 0 to 1 ms instead of 14 to 35. But the worker's request is sent in pieces cut along the statics' own per-item analysis (DefStatics), and finding none for the patched statics it went whole, about 70 ms more on the page. So the patch belongs inside that analysis: the edited item patched, and the program put back together by its own incremental step, so what is sent to the worker matches.

After that: the worker's answer, which still runs the whole program on the interpreter 1.5 to 2.4 s after a step and costs the page 50 to 60 ms to take in, though the compiled run already answered (report 050 step 4, partly done); a pause of 20 to 35 ms once a step after the answer, landing somewhere else each time, most likely the browser collecting the answer's garbage; and the step itself, 20 to 30 ms, a frame or so above 60 fps's 16.7.

Redoing it by hand, in small increments

The work can be redone in nine increments, ruler first, each a pull request small enough to read, and five of the ten ingredients need neither hazel-jit nor each other. There is a feeling on the team that some of this work should be redone by hand, to avoid the debt of machine-written code, in small increments. Some facts about what there is to redo: the branch, trial/694-view-jit, has {{n_commits}} commits from 11-06 to 11-10, adding {{src_add}} lines to src/ and removing {{src_del}}, in {{src_files}} files, with {{test_add}} lines of tests and {{scripts_add}} of scripts and CI. The compiler is {{jit_lines}} of those lines, in four modules, and {{jit_test}} of the test lines check that each compiled program agrees with the interpreter. Of the ten ingredients, {{untested}} came with no test of their own; the Firefox check, which drives the app and times it, was their only check. And {{n_fixups}} commits only fixed the one before, which did not compile, since nothing was compiled on the laptop and every type check ran on GitHub.

The order below puts the measuring first, then what helps any branch, then hazel-jit's pieces in the order they depend on each other. Each increment names the ingredient it carries; its size is the lines that ingredient's commits added to src/, a ceiling, since they include the timers each change was measured with.

{{tab_redo}}

Two things make a hand redo cheaper than the first time. The ruler already exists, so each increment can be measured the way the builds here were, and kept only if it helps. And the dead ends are known: samples sent as data made the answer larger and were turned off (build 11, report 047); two caches regressed the drag before they were fixed (builds 31 to 34). Neither needs to be tried again.

What next

  1. Ticket 721: when only a livelit's model changes, keep the statics the program had, with the records along the path from the edited item to the model (their terms and elaborated terms) and the program's elaboration given the new model. Build 42 measured what that could take away: 14 to 35 ms of statics, and the outline's 18 to 42 with it.
  2. Skip the worker's run when the compiled run has answered, or take its answer in without a whole view.
  3. Agree with the team on the order of the hand redo, and which increments go to dev first.

How this was put together

The numbers are the ones reports 045 to 051 measured, cited by build. The figures are drawn from each build's run of the Firefox check (scripts/jit_slides.py in jit.yml on GitHub's machines): its jit.json and jit.log, kept in report 050's and 051's data/runs/. A step's time is from the slider's click to the picture changing, steps 2 to 4 of the four (the first step of a slide pays first-call costs); the page's work after the picture is the sum of its top-level marks (view, calculate, update, decode, the outline, the comparison) from the picture to half a second after the worker's answer; a drag is 3 seconds of the neck slider. Each ingredient's code size is git show --numstat over the commits the ranked table names, in ~/hazel-694-jit, counting src/ without hazel-jit's own four modules (counted apart), and test/. The generator is 052/gen052.py; the numbers are in 052/data/numbers.json and in the page.
