    • ^ticket(681) trial/681-memo-big: the converters stop remembering small conversions. On fresh copies, Solitaire 30 to 8.6 ms and Mahjong solitaire 33 to 12; on the same objects again most within 5%, a few slower (Solitaire 3.32 to 3.79 ms), and Minesweeper slower on fresh copies (13 to 16 ms).

    • ^ticket(791) trial/791-labels-linear: two quadratic labeled-tuple helpers in Hazel's statics made linear; a build about 9% faster (warm 4.10–4.20 s to 3.77–3.79). LabeledTuple.re is shared Hazel code.
  • Why a build takes seconds when the jit is fast (Matthew's question, 11-27): the jit makes the rules run fast (Gnome world's step takes microseconds); building them is a one-time cost, and nearly all of it comes before hazel-jit compiles anything, as Hazel parses the rules and type-checks them. A CPU profile of Gnome world's build (^report(64)) puts 70% of it in the statics (Statics.re, inclusive), 31% in operations on types (Typ.meet, the common type of two branches, and matching labeled tuples' fields by name), 16% in LabeledTuple.re, 14% in the map of each term's information, and 28% in garbage collection (the parts overlap: each counts what it calls); the parser does not show, and hazel-jit's compiling is 0.5%, about 0.1 s. The statics are the editor's, built for live feedback: they record every term's type and context for the cursor's inspector, which a compile does not need. And Gnome world's rules are heavy in records (the gnome has 17 named fields), each field matched by name in every function; the birds' module, with the most and largest records, costs about three times the world's per character. In Node on a GitHub runner a build is about 5 s; in a browser on Matthew's laptop, 13.6 to 16.5 s this week. Together the trials make it about 8% faster (the figure); kept rules (890) skip it on a reload.
  • An experiment this morning, for numbers only, not to merge (^ticket(894)): every let analyzes its definition twice on the hazeljs branch (a probe for recursion, then the real pass), and three times when it might be recursive, so lets nested in definitions multiply, 2 to the depth; and a module binding analyzes its whole definition twice more. Two open PRs address this: #2603 (a let analyzed once unless it refers to itself; meet asked once per node, not three times) and #2717 (a module binding analyzes its definition once). Their three commits were ported to Core by hand onto the night's trials, as trial/1127-statics (ba32db7481; #2603's context index left out, as it conflicts). Gnome world's rules built in Node, all three libraries on one GitHub runner, three rounds (build timing run 46023240545): warm, 0.33 to 0.34 s against 2.87 to 3.19 s for the night's trials and 3.14 to 3.32 s for hazeljs, about 9 times as fast; the first build of a process 1.39 to 1.58 s against 3.94 to 4.44 and 4.34 to 5.09. Every piece of Gnome world gives the same answers as its JavaScript twin with the trial library, as with the night's (fresh timing run 46023247014: the gnome, the carpet, the cat tail, Clover, the boulders, picking up, the wind, the bunnies, frogs and birds). Hazel's own test suite on the trial was still running when this was written.
